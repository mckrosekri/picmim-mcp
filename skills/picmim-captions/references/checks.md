# Picmim caption checks

Picmim checks every caption it writes for a plan. Hold your own captions to the same bar.

## Hard checks (a caption that fails is not ready)

- No term from the brand's `rules.must_never`, plus Picmim's baseline: no unverified statistics or numeric claims, no made-up quotes, testimonials, endorsements or social proof, no guaranteed or specific outcomes, no comparisons with named competitors. "Helps you keep track of your money" is fine; an unsupported "saves you 500 a month" is not.
- Hashtags within the platform's range.
- At most 2 emojis.
- Within the platform's hard cap in characters.
- Product details (names, prices, sizes) unchanged from the brand profile or the user.
- Written in the caption language.

## Advice checks (fix them when you can)

- A weak first line: a label, a greeting, a service name or a vague benefit instead of a hook.
- One long block of text with no line breaks.
- The same opening as a neighbouring post in the same week or plan.
- Thin detail: nothing specific to this business.
- A quote or customer story that isn't saved anywhere in the brand profile.
- A phrase from the generic or corporate lists.

## The strictest generic set

Picmim always runs its strict generic check, which adds these phrases to the base generic list. They are already part of the banned list in the skill:

- best-in-class
- cutting-edge
- innovative solutions
- level up
- one-stop shop
- revolutionize
- seamless experience
- supercharge
- tailored solutions
- transform your business
- world-class

## Value posts and offer posts

- A value post gives the reader something useful or moving. A product may appear as a natural detail, never as an ad. Its only ask is to save, share or comment.
- An offer post may name the offer and one clear next step (book, call, visit, buy), with the exact link or phone number.
- Only add urgency ("ends Friday") when the user or the brand profile gave that date.

## On-image text

- Use only words the user gave or approved for the picture. At most 3 pieces of text (headline, supporting line, call to action), each at most 90 characters, and shorter is better: long text comes out garbled.
- Write the caption first, then pass the exact on-image strings to the image tool. Same language as the caption, and no dashes.
- On-image text names things the way customers know them, never by an internal product code.
